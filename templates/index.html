What is Schizophrenia?

A Brain Disorder

A chronic psychiatric disorder characterised by disturbances in thought, perception, emotion, and behaviour. Structural MRI reveals measurable neuroanatomical differences — particularly in the hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and ventricles.

Progressive Stages

The condition develops across distinct clinical stages — from a healthy baseline through early prodromal symptoms, active psychosis, and eventually chronic impairment. Stage-specific intervention significantly improves patient outcomes.

Manageable with Treatment

While there is no cure, antipsychotic therapy and psychosocial support substantially reduce symptom severity. Objective severity measures could one day help clinicians monitor progression, but this prototype has not been clinically validated.

The Four Severity Tiers

01

Healthy Controls

No Disorder

Neurotypical individuals serving as the biological baseline. Normal hippocampal volume, cortical thickness, and ventricular size form the reference for all cross-stage comparisons within the cohort.

02

Early Schizophrenia

Prodromal / First Episode

Onset of psychosis or prodromal symptoms — mild hallucinations, social withdrawal, and cognitive decline. Structural MRI changes are present but subtle; detectable only with deep learning-derived embeddings.

03

Moderate Schizophrenia

Active Psychosis

Persistent positive and negative symptoms with marked neuroanatomical changes — grey matter reduction, ventricular enlargement, and measurable hippocampal asymmetry are characteristic of this stage.

04

Chronic Schizophrenia

Long-term Illness

Years of illness with significant functional impairment. Negative symptoms dominate. Severe neuroanatomical deterioration is evident and patients typically require long-term antipsychotic management.

Symptom Clusters

Positive Symptoms — SAPS

  • Auditory and visual hallucinations
  • Persecutory and grandiose delusions
  • Disorganised thought and speech
  • Bizarre and disorganised behaviour
  • Inappropriate emotional responses

Negative Symptoms — SANS

  • Affective flattening (blunted emotions)
  • Alogia — poverty of speech
  • Avolition and social withdrawal
  • Anhedonia — inability to feel pleasure
  • Impaired attention and cognition
